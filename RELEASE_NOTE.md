Your time is the most stretched thing in the clinic, and we want to give some of it back. One piece of your day is writing up each doctor's note in plain language the patient can understand — every patient, every time. That's the piece we built something for.

What's new Monday. When a doctor's note is ready, you'll get a draft summary of it, already written in plain language for the patient. It also shows you what in the doctor's note each part is based on, so you can check it against the source as the tool continues to earn your trust. You read it, edit, and send.

What doesn't change. Nothing sends on its own. You are still the one who reviews and hits send — always. The draft is a starting point, not a decision. And if the system isn't confident a draft is safe, it won't hand it to you as ready — it'll flag it for you to write yourself, the way you do now.

How we'll know it's working. We're watching one thing: how long it takes you to get a patient-ready summary out. If this is working, that time drops — and that time goes back to your patients.

What we need from you. Tell us when it's wrong. If a draft misses something, over-explains, or just doesn't sound right, flag it. You know what a patient needs to hear better than any tool does, and the only way this gets better is your read on it.

Thank you — for the work, and for trying this with us.
